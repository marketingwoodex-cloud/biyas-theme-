The Canal-side residence began with a single request: 'make it quiet'. That sentence drove every decision.

## The courtyard

We removed a wall to connect the living floor to a small courtyard, then planted it with a single tree. Everything else was kept plain so the tree became the artwork.

## The joinery

Fumed oak throughout, with grain matched across doors. Handles were replaced by shadow gaps so nothing interrupts the surfaces.

## The light

No visible downlights in the main rooms. Light comes from coves, from behind the joinery and from a handful of table lamps. Each room has three scenes — morning, evening and 'guests' — on a single keypad by the door, and the family uses all of them.

## The kitchen

The only room where efficiency won over quiet. A long island in fumed oak faces the courtyard, with the working wall behind it hidden behind pocket doors that close after dinner. The extractor is in the ceiling, the sockets are in the drawers, and nothing sits on the worktop unless someone is cooking.

## What we would do again

Give the courtyard tree its own budget line. Spend on the things people touch — handles, taps, the stair rail — and save on the things they only look at. And keep the palette to four materials, no matter how many the samples table tempts you with.
