Storage is the difference between a small apartment that feels calm and one that feels crowded. The trick is to make it disappear.

1. **Full-height walls.** Floor to ceiling, wall to wall, painted the same colour as the room.
2. **The furniture wall.** One volume that contains kitchen, pantry and utility.
3. **Beds with drawers,** not boxes — easier to reach, no lifting.
4. **Door-height wardrobes** with flush doors and push latches.
5. **The hidden desk,** folded inside a cabinet for the days you work from home.

## Why one colour matters

Storage disappears when its surfaces read as walls. Painting full-height joinery the same colour as the room — or specifying a veneer that matches the floor — turns twelve cupboards into one calm plane. Handles break the spell, which is why we use push latches or a continuous shadow gap instead.

## The numbers behind it

In a recent 90 m² apartment the furniture wall alone holds 14 linear metres of storage — a pantry, a washing machine, a fridge, coats and a fold-out guest bed — inside a volume 600 mm deep. The bedrooms gained 40 % more usable floor by replacing freestanding wardrobes with flush built-ins that run to the ceiling.

## Start with an inventory

Before we draw a single cupboard we ask clients to count: shoes, coats, suitcases, books, serving dishes, the things that only come out at Eid. Storage designed from a real list is always smaller than storage designed from anxiety — and it always has room left over.
