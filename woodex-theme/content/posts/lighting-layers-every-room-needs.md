A grid of downlights is not a lighting design. It is a way to light a floor evenly while making the people in the room look tired.

## Ambient

Soft, indirect light that sets the base level: coves, wall washers, large shades.

## Task

Light where work happens — under cabinets, beside beds, over desks. Task light should be switched separately.

## Accent

The layer that creates depth: a picture light, a wash on a textured wall, a glow inside a bookshelf. Keep it to a few moments per room.

## Where the switches go

Put every layer on its own circuit, and you will use all three. The ambient layer belongs on the switch by the door; task lights are switched where the task happens; accent lights go on the dimmer you reach for when guests arrive. A room with one switch that turns on everything will always be lit like a corridor.

## Colour temperature and dimming

Use 2700 K in living spaces and bedrooms, 3000 K in kitchens and bathrooms, and never mix them in one view. Specify dimmable drivers even where you think you won't dim — the cost difference is small and a room without dimming has exactly one mood.

## The mistake almost every ceiling makes

A symmetrical grid of downlights, spaced for the room rather than for what's in it. Light should land on walls, surfaces and objects, not on the floor between them. If you inherit a grid, switch half of it off, add two table lamps and a picture light, and watch the room recover.
