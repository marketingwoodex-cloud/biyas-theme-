“Shafaq-e-Tishnagi” — the thirst-quenched dawn — gathers the couplets that grew alongside the thread folios: poems of fields, rain, courtyards and the hours between lamps. Each section opens with a plate, so the verse and the weave keep each other company on the page as they do in the house.
