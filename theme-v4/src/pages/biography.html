The story so far

A life told in
chapters.

Every biography claims beginnings; hers are verifiable in works. A Punjabi poet, fiction writer, social worker and columnist from Lahore, she brings a rare continuity to every part of the practice: the line of a poem, the direction of thread and the emotional pull of colour.

Her cultural leadership extends beyond the studio — Chairperson of Punjabi Virsa and President of Arts & Culture, working for the preservation and forward movement of Punjabi creative expression. The chapters below are dated as the record confirms them; the ones still being written are left open on purpose.

Chapter I — Lahore

The city as first teacher

Lahore's walls, its truck art, its Nastaliq signage and its evening qawwali — an education no academy could arrange. The city taught proportion before anyone called it design.

Chapter II — The pen

Letters before pictures

Calligraphy came first: the discipline of writing a phrase until the hand knows it by heart. Gold pigment followed, then dark grounds to set it burning.

Chapter III — The thread

The Third Art is born

Pen, brush — and then a third instrument. Carrying calligraphic gesture into woven fibre became the signature practice now known through the house as The Third Art.

Chapter IV — The verse

Punjabi, out loud

Three collections of Punjabi poetry — ghazals and nazams, documented by Folk Punjab — returning always to the many dimensions of ishq: love as devotion, longing, memory. The house began to gather monthly mehfils around it.

Chapter V — The wider room

A cultural voice

Chairperson of Punjabi Virsa and President of Arts & Culture — and, in a 2024 Qasim Ali Shah Foundation interview, introduced as poet, fiction writer, artist and businesswoman: a portrait of a creator whose work is as expansive as it is personal.

Chapter VI — Today

The house with many rooms

Studio, gallery, salon and sanctuary: commissions travel to collectors and interiors, while the front door stays open to Lahore, eleven to seven.

Today

In the studio

Mornings belong to thread; afternoons to the pen. Commissions and the current Sacred Light chapter occupy the easels, while the monthly mehfil keeps the calendar honest — art is finished in public here.

Tomorrow

On the loom of plans

A travelling exhibition of The Third Art, a collected book of Verha verse with original folios, and a school corner where Lahore's children can pull their first thread. Slowly — the way everything here is made.
